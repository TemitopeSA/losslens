The problem

Loss reasons in the CRM are a rep's guess from a dropdown. Real win/loss research is slow, expensive and covers a handful of deals.

The product

A Salesforce trigger invites the buyer to a 15-minute AI interview. The real reason flows back to the CRM, a live dashboard and Ask AI.

Why Great Question

8 of the 11 building blocks are already live. LossLens adds a CRM trigger, a win/loss view and a write-back, and opens a budget held by revenue leaders.

The problem

Companies record why they lost. They rarely know why.

When a deal closes lost, the rep picks a reason from a picklist, usually Price or No decision. That field then feeds pipeline reviews, pricing debates and roadmap arguments. Nobody asked the buyer.

The teams that do ask the buyer run win/loss as a project: an outside firm or a researcher's spare calendar, a quarterly readout, a dozen hand-picked deals and a slide deck that is out of date by the next board meeting.

Great Question already has every ingredient to make this continuous: the CRM connection, a moderator that works around the clock, incentives, and a repository that keeps evidence searchable. LossLens connects them to one event that every B2B company has: Closed Lost.

Walkthrough

The prototype, screen by screen.

Every screen sits inside the Great Question app shell and uses its own terms: Studies, Candidates, Repository, Incentives, Studio. The demo workspace is a fictional spend-management company called Ledgerly.

STEP 1 · TRIGGER

It starts where revenue teams already live.

A new Triggers tab on the existing Salesforce integration. Pick which lost deals qualify, who gets invited, how long to wait and which study runs. The footer estimates volume and incentive spend before anything goes live.

What this showsSetup is a settings change for an admin, not a research project.
STEP 2 · GUARDRAILS

Sales stays in control.

Deal owners get a Slack message with Hold or Let it run. Each contact is invited at most once per 90 days, unsubscribes are honored, and a monthly incentive cap protects the budget.

What this showsAnswers the first objection a sales leader will raise: "don't email my buyers without asking."
STEP 3 · STUDY

An always-on study, set up once.

A research goal, a six-question discussion guide and a moderator set to Balanced flexibility. Concealed identity is on, so the buyer talks to a neutral "Moderator" and never sees Ledgerly's branding.

What this showsA neutral interviewer gets more candid answers than the rep who just lost the deal.
STEP 4 · AUTOMATION

Watch one deal run end to end.

"Simulate a lost deal" plays the whole sequence for Acme Freight, an $84,000 opportunity the rep logged as Price: Slack heads-up, unbranded invite, a 14-minute interview, then the Salesforce update.

What this showsThe full loop runs with no researcher involved.
STEP 5 · INTERVIEW

The buyer says price. The moderator asks what happened.

At 05:48 the VP Finance says it came down to price. The moderator asks what happened right before price became the deciding factor. At 06:24 the real reason surfaces: no native NetSuite sync, so the extra implementation work made Ledgerly feel expensive.

What this showsThe interview finds the reason behind the stated reason, and every claim links to a timestamp.
STEP 6 · WRITE-BACK

The real reason lands back in the CRM.

Two fields update on the Opportunity: Interview_Status__c and GQ_Primary_Loss_Reason__c. The rep's original reason is kept untouched, so both views can be reported side by side.

What this showsResearch becomes structured revenue data that RevOps can report on.
STEP 7 · WIN/LOSS VIEW

One page for the CRO and the head of product.

Pipeline lost by real reason, competitor mentions, and synthesized insights with a quote, the buyer's role, the deal size and a clip. Share to Slack, build a highlight reel or export.

What this showsA recurring, evidence-backed answer to "why are we losing?"
STEP 8 · ASK AI AND MCP

Ask from anywhere, with citations.

"Why did we lose to Spendwise this quarter, and what should product prioritise?" The answer cites interview timestamps and ends with a recommendation: ship native NetSuite sync, cited in 6 of 9 Spendwise losses, worth $388k.

What this showsThe same evidence works in Claude, Cursor or ChatGPT through the existing MCP server.

Why this matters for Great Question

A new buyer, a new revenue line, and a reason to renew.

New budget

Sells to the revenue org.

Today the buyer is research, product and design. Win/loss is owned by the CRO, RevOps and product marketing, and is usually paid for with outside agency fees. LossLens puts Great Question in front of a second budget holder at every account that already syncs Salesforce.

Usage revenue

Grows with interviews, not seats.

Each triggered interview is metered AI moderation plus incentives flowing through the wallet. That fits the move toward agentic research, where an agent runs more studies than any seat holder could.

Proof of value

Research that ties to dollars.

Every insight carries a deal amount. Research leaders can show which roadmap decisions came from lost pipeline, the "close to the money" positioning Ned has argued for, and that keeps champions funded at renewal.

Defensibility

Hard for point tools to copy.

AI-interview startups can run the conversation. They don't have the CRM trigger, governance, incentives, recruitment and a cross-study repository in one place. LossLens turns that breadth into a visible advantage.

Risks and how the design answers them

The objections, handled up front.

ObjectionDesign answer
"Don't contact my buyers without asking."Slack heads-up to the deal owner with a 24-hour hold. Triggers can exclude segments, renewals and named accounts.
Buyer fatigue and brand riskOne invite per contact per 90 days across all Great Question studies. Unsubscribes and do-not-contact honored from Salesforce. Neutral, unbranded invite.
Low response ratesIncentive per completed interview, a 15-minute format and no scheduling. The prototype assumes 45%; the pilot will test it.
Trust in AI findingsEvery summary and Ask AI answer cites interview timestamps. The rep's reason is kept beside the buyer's so leaders can compare both.
Spend controlMonthly incentive cap with live usage, plus the existing wallet balance alerts.
CRM coverageSalesforce first because Great Question already syncs it. HubSpot is the next connector.
